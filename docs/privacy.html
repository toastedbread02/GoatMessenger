privacy

Privacy policy

Short version: GoatMessenger encrypts everything before it leaves your device, collects no more than it needs to connect two goats, and can erase all of it with one button. You can verify every claim in the source code.

1. Who runs this

GoatMessenger is an independent open-source project, built and maintained by @toastedbread02 (see business details). There is no company, no advertising and no data broker behind it.

2. The one principle: you own your relay

GoatMessenger does not run a central server that reads everyone's data. You (or the friend you trust) run the tiny relay program. That means the list below describes what a relay stores — and since you can host your own, you can check it physically, on your own disk. If you use someone else's relay, that person could see the same limited list.

3. What we collect (the whole list)

  • Account basics: your goat name, display name (≤40 chars), short bio (≤120 chars), a profile hue, profile photo and banner (compressed to ~220 KB/~420 KB), and the date you joined.
  • Passphrase check: a random salt and a hash of a token derived from your passphrase (scrypt). Your passphrase, and the keys it protects, never leave your device.
  • Public key bundle so strangers can find and verify you, plus one encrypted blob ("identity backup") that only your passphrase can open — so you can log in on a new device.
  • Contact list: who has sent or accepted a request with whom.
  • Message envelopes: only ciphertext — encrypted blobs the relay cannot read — queued per conversation, capped at the last 5,000.
  • Attachments: the encrypted files you send are parked on the relay until the recipient fetches them.
  • A session token per signed-in device, and a temporary in-memory IP check for rate limiting that is never logged or written.
  • Online/offline presence and typing indicators while the app is connected.

4. What nobody ever sees

Message and call contents, attachments in the clear, your passphrase, your private keys, and your key backup are end-to-end encrypted and meaningless to any relay. What a relay can see is metadata: who talks to whom, roughly how much, and when. We say this plainly rather than pretend otherwise.

5. The web app (running in your browser)

If you choose "Continue in browser", messages, keys and preferences are stored in your browser only (IndexedDB + localStorage) — never synced anywhere, and never readable by us. The website itself sets no cookies and runs no analytics; see the cookie & storage policy.

6. Third-party code and services (the full audit)

We audit nothing quietly — here is every third-party component:

  • noble-hashes / noble-curves (MIT) — vendored cryptography code used to build the X25519/Ed25519/scrypt operations. Runs locally; phones nobody.
  • WebRTC calls. Media is peer-to-peer. To find a route, apps contact Google's free public STUN servers (stun.l.google.com) by default — they see your public IP at call time and nothing else. Add your own TURN server in Settings → Network to stop even that.
  • GitHub hosts this site (GitHub Pages) and the release downloads. GitHub will see your IP when you load pages, as any web host does.
  • That's the entire list. No analytics, no ad networks, no trackers, no fonts CDNs, no support widgets.

7. Only what's needed, and not a byte more

Every field in the signup payload is checked server-side and anything extra is discarded. No email is ever requested — there is nowhere to send spam if we can't see your inbox. Guest accounts get a random name, no email, and only the minimal subset of profile data.

8. Deleting your data

In the app: Settings → Profile → Delete account & data. Type your goat name to confirm, and the relay erases your account, profile, key backup, contact rows, message envelopes and uploaded files — your device wipes its local copy too. Your friend's chat loses your messages (they keep their own copies of their own history). You can also just clear the local history per device, or sign out, which forgets the saved login.

9. Kids

GoatMessenger is not aimed at children. Signing up requires confirming you are at least 13 (older where your local law says so). If a child under that age somehow has an account, use the deletion button above or contact us and we'll help erase it.

10. Contact

Questions, deletion requests, data leaks, Wednesday goat pictures: open a GitHub issue. It is the fastest way to reach the one human maintaining this.
